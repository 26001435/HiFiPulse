ABOUT HIFIPULSE

Find the problem first. Fix what matters.

HiFiPulse is a student-facing project exploring how everyday network measurements can help people navigate campus—and make unequal access harder to ignore. Our starting point is Universiti Malaya—PASUM, KK11 and KK12. The broader idea is to diagnose connectivity gaps before spending on fixes, and check whether those fixes actually help.

OUR MISSION

Make connectivity useful, visible and fair.

Students should not have to guess which study space will support a submission, a video call or a lecture. We turn individual measurements into a more useful view of campus, while being clear about gaps in the evidence. HiFiPulse is not an official university network monitor. Our proposed pilot asks whether evidence can guide practical improvements from the least costly option to the infrastructure upgrades that truly need investment.

THREE OBJECTIVES

What we are working toward

01

Better digital equity

Help reveal where a poor connection makes coursework harder. Repeated measurements, sample counts and responsible interpretation can give students and campus decision-makers a more grounded starting point for improving access. A mobile-data fallback should never be the only way to participate.

02

Less time searching for a place to study

Compare connection quality around libraries, cafés, study cubes and residence areas before moving across campus. Suggested spots need repeated, nearby tests before they receive a strong-coverage label. We do not claim to know whether a seat is free.

03

More informed connectivity decisions

Explain the difference between Wi-Fi signal, network congestion, cellular reception and upstream outages. The guide offers practical ways to reduce avoidable traffic and protect personal data, without placing the burden of infrastructure failures on students.

A CLOSER LOOK

What does a test actually tell us?

Open a question to see how we interpret a result.

Is one fast test enough to recommend a study space?

Not on its own. Speeds change with time and load. We look for repeated, nearby measurements and show when there is not enough evidence. We cannot tell whether a seat is available.

Does a pin prove which room someone was in?

No. GPS can drift, particularly indoors. Place names are self-reported; a map pin shows an approximate recorded location, not a verified room or building.

Will my report appear on another device?

Speed tests only appear across devices when the external results service saves them and its published feed is reachable. Study-space suggestions and manual dead-zone reports currently stay in this browser.

HOW IT WORKS

Measurements first. Claims second.

Measure

A browser test records download, upload, ping and jitter. The student selects the network being tested and may provide a place name and approximate location.

Compare

Map points and historical medians give context for a specific area and time. Network labels are self-reported; low sample counts and single tests should not be treated as guarantees.

Learn

Study-space suggestions and the Wi-Fi guide help people put the data to work. Student-reported dead zones are clearly separated from measured test results.

THE BIGGER IDEA

Diagnose before buying.

A slow connection in a crowded hall does not tell us what failed. It might be a weak signal, competition for airtime, an access-point placement problem or an upstream limit. A browser speed test alone cannot distinguish those causes.

Our PITRAM proposal turns HiFiPulse into an evidence-first cycle: measure → explain → try the least costly appropriate response → independently verify. Start with observations by place, network and time, including failed attempts. Investigate recurring gaps under controlled conditions. Try guidance and approved low-cost options where they make sense; ask campus IT to assess access points or backhaul when evidence points to an infrastructure problem. We would record costs and negative results, not just successes.

  1. 01 / MEASURE

    Opt-in browser tests exist now; proposed fixed-position readings would add repeat context in a permitted hall.

  2. 02 / EXPLAIN

    A proposed crowd study would compare signal, latency and throughput at fixed positions and occupancy levels, rather than blaming one cause from a speed reading.

  3. 03 / RESPOND

    Use practical guidance and the rule-based task planner first where useful; investigate proposed low-cost trials only with permission. Institutional upgrades remain an IT decision.

  4. 04 / VERIFY

    Re-test the same conditions, including failures and effects on other seats. If a response does not help, say so.

THE ACCOMPANYING IDEA / EUREKA@KK12

WaveLens: a physics experiment, not a claimed fix.

Our proposed passive Fresnel-zone plate brings the measure → explain → trial → verify loop into the physical world. Alternating open and covered rings explore diffraction and interference at Wi-Fi frequencies. The goal is to test whether a carefully designed plate can help a measured weak position without harming adjacent seats.

Not built or validated in this repository: there is no documented plate, measured signal gain, confirmed sub-RM5 cost or UM installation. HiFiPulse cannot identify RF causes from a speed reading alone. A controlled bench study and permission are needed before any campus trial.

PROPOSED PILOT / NOT DEPLOYED

From student tool to careful experiments

These are research ideas from our PITRAM proposal—not features currently operating on campus. Any hall trial needs consent and approval from the relevant lecturers, UM IT and facilities teams.

Study why a crowded lecture hall struggles

At approved fixed positions, a proposed study would compare Wi-Fi signal strength, ping and throughput across different occupancy levels. Headcounts and other conditions would be recorded; no cameras or other students’ device identifiers are proposed. A change in speed alone would not prove crowd absorption.

Test small, low-cost responses

Bench tests would first check whether low-duty-cycle ESP32 sensing could work with harvested indoor energy, and whether a steerable reflector helps a weak spot without worsening another. Power budgets, parts, labour and any actual signal effects are unverified. An optional local offline-content node would require content rights and a security review; it cannot handle live submissions.

Check who benefits—and who is left behind

Students should not need to buy mobile data to overcome a campus gap. The proposed pilot would compare access patterns without recording individual finances or publishing personal routes. This work relates to SDG 4 (quality education), SDG 9 (infrastructure) and SDG 10 (reduced inequalities); the prototype alone does not achieve those goals.

WHERE WE ARE NOW

A prototype with clear boundaries

The map depends on an external published results feed. Study-space suggestions and dead-zone reports submitted through their forms currently stay on the device where they were made. A shared provider comparison needs the external data service to save the provider field. We have not built live seat counts, network-capacity predictions, the proposed hall sensors, reflector or WaveLens plate, an OfflineNode, or an official IT dashboard.

Next steps include a shared, moderated reporting service, broader campus measurements and privacy-conscious ways to share aggregate evidence with students and campus IT. These are plans, not available features.
