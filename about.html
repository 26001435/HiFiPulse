ABOUT HIFIPULSE

Better access begins with better evidence.

HiFiPulse is a student-facing project exploring how everyday network measurements can help people navigate campus—and make unequal access harder to ignore. Our initial focus is Universiti Malaya, particularly PASUM, KK11 and KK12.

OUR MISSION

Make connectivity useful, visible and fair.

Students should not have to guess which study space will support a submission, a video call or a lecture. We turn individual measurements into a more useful view of campus, while being clear about gaps in the evidence. HiFiPulse is not an official university network monitor.

THREE OBJECTIVES

What we are working toward

01

Better digital equity

Help reveal where a poor connection makes coursework harder. Repeated measurements, sample counts and responsible interpretation can give students and campus decision-makers a more grounded starting point for improving access. A mobile-data fallback should never be the only way to participate.

02

Less time searching for a place to study

Compare connection quality around libraries, cafés, study cubes and residence areas before moving across campus. Suggested spots need repeated, nearby tests before they receive a strong-coverage label. We do not claim to know whether a seat is free.

03

More informed connectivity decisions

Explain the difference between Wi-Fi signal, network congestion, cellular reception and upstream outages. The guide offers practical ways to reduce avoidable traffic and protect personal data, without placing the burden of infrastructure failures on students.

HOW IT WORKS

Measurements first. Claims second.

Measure

A browser test records download, upload, ping and jitter. The student selects the network being tested and may provide a place name and approximate location.

Compare

Map points and historical medians give context for a specific area and time. Network labels are self-reported; low sample counts and single tests should not be treated as guarantees.

Learn

Study-space suggestions and the Wi-Fi guide help people put the data to work. Student-reported dead zones are clearly separated from measured test results.

WHERE WE ARE NOW

A prototype with clear boundaries

The map depends on an external published results feed. Study-space suggestions and dead-zone reports submitted through their forms currently stay on the device where they were made. A shared provider comparison needs the external data service to save the provider field. We have not built live seat counts, network-capacity predictions or an official IT dashboard.

Next steps include a shared, moderated reporting service, broader campus measurements and privacy-conscious ways to share aggregate evidence with students and campus IT. These are plans, not available features.
