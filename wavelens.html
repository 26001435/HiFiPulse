EUREKA@KK12 CONCEPT · NOT DEPLOYED

Meet WaveLens.

HiFiPulse helps us find a recurring connection problem. WaveLens asks a second question: could a low-cost, passive Fresnel zone plate help at one specific position—without making nearby positions worse?

It is a proposed experiment, not a proven Wi-Fi fix. We have not documented a built plate, a measured gain or a campus installation. The drawing is a design illustration, not a photograph of a working device.

THE RESEARCH LOOP

Measure. Explain. Trial. Verify.

One speed reading cannot tell us why a hall is slow. A plate is only a candidate response after the problem is understood.

  1. 01 / ObserveUse opt-in HiFiPulse tests to look for repeat patterns by approximate place, network and time.
  2. 02 / DiagnoseSeparate signal, congestion and upstream limits. Browser speed tests do not measure Wi-Fi signal strength (RSSI).
  3. 03 / TrialWith permissions and calculated geometry, compare a plate with no plate and a same-size control.
  4. 04 / VerifyRepeat at fixed positions, measure RSSI and throughput separately, and check adjacent seats for losses.

ASASI WAVE OPTICS

From path difference to a testable idea.

Alternating concentric zones select parts of a wavefront that could add more favourably at a chosen focus. It is a way to explore diffraction and interference; Young’s slits and Newton’s rings are helpful analogies for superposition, but they are not proof of radio performance in a real room.

Plane-wave zone boundaryrₙ = √[nλf + (nλ/2)²]λ ≈ 5.79 cm at 5.18 GHz. For a focus 15 cm behind the plate, four boundaries are approximately 9.8, 14.4, 18.3 and 21.9 cm: about 43.9 cm across. These dimensions apply only to this idealised geometry.

A finite-distance source and receiver need a different phase calculation. “15 cm focal length” does not mean placing the plate 15 cm from a router to improve a distant seat. The plate redistributes energy, not amplifies it; indoor reflections, channel changes and alignment can defeat the idea.

WHAT IS REAL TODAY?

Separate the tool from the hypothesis.

Browser prototype

HiFiPulse can run an opt-in test and show local history and approximate map observations. Shared publication depends on a separately deployed data service.

Proposed physical experiment

WaveLens has not been documented as built or validated here. “Under RM5” is a materials-cost target, not a verified total. No gain or throughput improvement is claimed.

Before a campus trial

Build and measure a controlled prototype, confirm the actual Wi-Fi channel, record costs and off-axis effects, and obtain appropriate lecturer and UM IT/facilities permission. A null result is still useful.
